# Analysis: a distribution theory for Q(Σ_t, P) and ΔQ

**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Author:** Claude (research assistant), for Artur Sepp
**Reviewed:** `2026-08-13-thoughts_cluster_distribution.md`. This note answers its two TODOs: (1) what literature exists for a stationary distribution of the partition Q(Σ_t, P) as the asset count grows, and (2) whether the programme ΔΣ_t → ΔQ_t → min_P Var(ΔQ) is feasible and what literature covers it.

**Summary verdict up front.** The programme is feasible, and more of it is provable than the note conjectures — but the two conjectures split unevenly. The stationary distribution of Q_t exists under weak conditions and is a short lemma, not a research question; the research content is *characterising* that distribution (concentrated vs diffuse), and there the recovery-threshold literature supplies the machinery. The asymptotic law of ΔΣ_t is essentially known (EWMA filtering theory plus the delta method). The asymptotic law of ΔQ_t is the genuinely novel and hard step, and it is tractable in exactly one regime — the well-separated block-model regime via a boundary-flip analysis — which happens to be the regime that yields the noise-floor calibration of δ from the 2026-08-13 roadmap review as a corollary. The variance-minimisation objective has a known degeneracy that must be fixed by a constraint before anything is optimised. No existing paper assembles these pieces for rolling correlation clustering; the ingredients sit in four separate literatures.

---

## 1. Restating the framework, with one correction of scope

The note's objects: Σ_t an EWMA covariance/correlation with span N, optionally PC-1-removed; a clustering map Q(Σ_t, P) with parameter set P (in our pipeline P = {linkage, distance transform, cutoff fraction, smoother type, δ or λ_s}); returns iid with AR(1)/GARCH/IGARCH volatility parameterised by Θ; the objects of study ΔΣ_t and ΔQ_t; the goal min_P Var(ΔQ).

One conflation to undo before the literature mapping, because the two limits have different answers. "Number of assets increases to infinity ⇒ stationary distribution of Q" mixes **stationarity in t** (a property of the Markov process Σ_t at fixed dimension d) with the **large-d limit** (a concentration or phase-transition statement). Both are meaningful; they are different theorems with different literatures. Section 2 treats them separately.

## 2. TODO 1: the stationary distribution of Q(Σ_t, P)

### 2.1 Stationarity in time is a lemma, not a conjecture

The EWMA recursion Σ_t = λΣ_{t−1} + (1−λ) x_t x_t′ is an iterated random function with contraction factor λ < 1. For strictly stationary ergodic returns with E‖xx′‖ < ∞, the process Σ_t has a unique stationary law and is geometrically ergodic (Diaconis–Freedman iterated random functions; for GARCH-type x_t this is standard). Since Q_t = f(Σ_t) for a measurable map into the *finite* lattice of partitions of d assets, Q_t inherits strict stationarity immediately: its stationary distribution is the pushforward of the stationary law of Σ_t. So under iid returns, or GARCH with α+β < 1, existence holds with a three-line proof. One Θ-regime caveat worth stating in any writeup: under IGARCH the returns are strictly stationary but have infinite unconditional variance (Nelson 1990), so Σ_t still admits a strict stationary law but moment-based arguments (and Var(ΔQ) read literally as a variance) need care; the RiskMetrics EWMA *is* the IGARCH(1,1) filter, which is why this case belongs in the theory even though it breaks second moments.

### 2.2 The research content: when is the stationary law concentrated?

This is where the literature lives, in four strands, none of which does the rolling-EWMA case:

1. **Variable clustering with recovery guarantees (the closest statistics).** Bunea, Giraud and coauthors develop G-block covariance models — the population covariance is block-structured — with minimax-optimal *exact recovery* results: the CORD algorithm ([Bunea–Giraud–Luo–Royer–Verzelen, "Model assisted variable clustering", Annals of Statistics 2020](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-48/issue-1/Model-assisted-variable-clustering-Minimax-optimal-recovery-and-algorithms/10.1214/18-AOS1794.full); [arXiv:1508.01939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.01939)) and the convex [PECOK](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/PECOK:-a-convex-optimization-approach-to-variable-Bunea-Giraud/d87e22296b9dc12dfa02706095cbbf52a1641149) recover the true partition with high probability iff the within-vs-between block separation exceeds a threshold of order √(log d / n). Translated to our setting: with n_eff ≈ N fixed and d growing, the noise floor does *not* vanish, so the stationary law of Q_t concentrates on the population partition when cluster separation clears the threshold and stays diffuse below it. This is the exact formal home for "under specific conditions there must be a stationary distribution": the conditions are separation conditions, and the interesting object is the **phase diagram in (separation, d/N)**.
2. **Random matrix theory for EWMA covariance.** The d/N_eff-finite regime for exponentially weighted estimators is worked out in [Pafka–Potters–Kondor 2004, "Exponential weighting and random-matrix-theory-based filtering"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0402573.pdf) — the EWMA analogue of Marchenko–Pastur with an effective quality factor q. Together with the spiked-model results surveyed in [Potters–Bouchaud, "Financial applications of random matrix theory"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24129883_Financial_Applications_of_Random_Matrix_Theory_Old_Laces_and_New_Pieces), this is the right language for the PC-1 removal: the market mode is the dominant spike; detoning strips it so the block structure becomes the leading eigenstructure, and BBP-type thresholds say when block spikes are detectable above the bulk at all. Eigenvector fluctuation results for [sample correlation matrices in spiked models](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8026145/) quantify how noisy the retained directions are.
3. **Stability properties of the clustering map itself.** [Carlsson–Mémoli, JMLR 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/carlsson10a.html) prove that among linkage methods only single linkage is stable and convergent in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense; complete and average linkage are not, and Ward sits outside their axioms entirely (see also the [Gromov–Hausdorff stability of linkage methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5068) follow-up). Consequence for us: a distributional theory for *Ward* partitions cannot go through generic metric-space stability. It must go through the block-model route, where the population dendrogram is deterministic and merge heights concentrate. This is also the theoretically honest response to Mika's standing Ward-metric objection: the theory holds Ward fixed as the estimator used in production and derives its behaviour under a generative model, rather than claiming axiomatic stability Ward does not have.
4. **Partition-valued dynamics.** [Dynamic stochastic block models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11222-017-9788-9) (Ludkin–Eckley–Neal 2018; Matias–Miele) define explicit Markov chains on partitions with stationary distributions — the closest existing formalisation of a "stationary distribution of Q_t", but with the partition as the *latent state* driving observations. Our setting inverts this: the partition is a deterministic functional of a noisy estimate. Bayesian dependent-partition processes (dependent Dirichlet processes and relatives) are the other formal home if one ever wants Q_t itself modelled as random. For the pure large-d limit of a partition, the Kingman paintbox / exchangeable-random-partitions framework is the canonical formalism: under block-exchangeability the normalised cluster-size distribution converges — a clean way to state "the shape of Q stabilises as d → ∞".

Finance-specific: [Marti et al., "Clustering financial time series: how long is enough?" (IJCAI 2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.04017) gives convergence rates of correlation-based clustering in sample length for financial DGPs — the T-direction cousin of the d-direction results above, and the right citation for the paper's motivation section.

**Answer to TODO 1.** Existence: provable, easy, worth one lemma. Characterisation: no paper does it for rolling EWMA correlation clustering; the assembly of (1)+(2) above into a concentration statement with a phase diagram is novel and feasible. That assembly, not existence, is the claimable object.

## 3. TODO 2: feasibility of ΔΣ → ΔQ → min Var(ΔQ)

### 3.1 The law of ΔΣ_t: feasible and mostly known

The one-step identity is exact and already informative: ΔΣ_t = (1−λ)(x_t x_t′ − Σ_{t−1}). Conditionally on the past, ΔΣ_t has an explicit mean-reverting drift toward the true covariance and a Wishart-type innovation whose covariance is the fourth-moment tensor of returns. Three known layers give the "asymptotic law" the note conjectures:

- **Fixed d, large N.** vech(Σ_t) is an AR(1)-type process with coefficient λ. For iid returns with finite fourth moments the stationary fluctuation variance is Var(vech(xx′))·(1−λ)/(1+λ) = Var(vech(xx′))/N exactly (with λ = 1 − 2/(N+1)), which is the n_eff = N result. The delta method maps this to correlations ([Neudecker–Wesselman 1990](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002437959090363H); [general-conditions version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379586901503)), giving SE(ρ̂) ≈ (1−ρ²)/√N under Gaussianity.
- **GARCH DGP.** This is precisely Nelson–Foster asymptotic filtering theory: [misspecified ARCH filters I](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/030440769290065Y) and [II](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0132), and the [multivariate version](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0304407694016798), derive the asymptotic distribution of the measurement error of EWMA-type variance filters under a GARCH data-generating process. Θ enters through conditional kurtosis and vol-of-vol persistence: fat tails inflate Var(vech(xx′)), so the correlation noise floor carries a kurtosis multiplier relative to the Gaussian formula. This is a directly usable refinement of the δ-calibration from the roadmap review: **under GARCH the calibrated δ should exceed the Gaussian (1−ρ²)/√N by a factor derivable from Θ** — a testable prediction, since the three universes have different kurtosis.
- **Large d.** The Pafka–Kondor–Potters EWMA-RMT results above.

One more exact and useful piece: because Σ_t is AR(1) with coefficient λ, the variance of the *between-estimation-date* change over k periods is 2(1−λ^k)·Var_stationary. At weekly λ with monthly estimation (k ≈ 4.33), 1−λ^k ≈ 5.4%: the per-transition distance noise is about √(2·0.054) ≈ 0.33 of the stationary noise. So the framework quantifies how churn should scale with rebalancing frequency — a second testable prediction, for free.

### 3.2 The law of ΔQ_t: hard in general, tractable in the regime that matters

Q is a piecewise-constant, partition-valued function of Σ, so there is no delta method: the map is non-differentiable exactly at the configurations that generate churn. Full generality is out of reach and I would not pursue it. The tractable route:

**Boundary-flip analysis in the separated regime.** Under a G-block population model, asset i carries a margin m_i: the gap between its distance to its own cluster and to the nearest alternative at the cut (in population, from the block parameters). A membership flip requires the noise in that gap to cross the margin, so P(flip_i) ≈ Φ(−m_i/(√2·σ_d)) with σ_d the per-transition distance noise from 3.1, and expected churn is Σ_i P(flip_i). Adding the partition bonus δ makes the flip condition hysteretic: P(flip_i) ≈ Φ(−(m_i+δ)/(√2·σ_d)). Three things fall out at once. First, an analytic expected-churn formula as a function of (Θ, N, P, margin distribution) — the "asymptotic law of ΔQ" in the mean, with the distribution of churn following from independence approximations across boundary assets (Poisson-binomial). Second, the empirical crux (5% information innovation producing 24% monthly reassignment) is *explained*: the observed churn identifies the mass of assets with margins below ~σ_d, which is measurable from the dendrogram. Third, the roadmap review's δ = z·SE rule is the corollary: δ chosen to push false-flip probability to a target α is δ = z_α·√2·σ_d − m, i.e. the noise-floor rule, now derived rather than postulated. The known theoretical anchor for "stability is boundary mass" is exactly this shape: [Ben-David–von Luxburg–Pál 2006](https://www.tml.cs.uni-tuebingen.de/team/luxburg/publications/BenLuxPal06.pdf) and the boundary-mass results surveyed in [von Luxburg 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.1075) ([related boundary paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221497206_Relating_Clustering_Stability_to_Properties_of_Cluster_Boundaries)).

The honest caveat: for Ward the margin is a functional of the whole tree, not a local distance gap. The clean proposition is for the flat cut on a block model (merge heights concentrate, so the population tree is deterministic and margins are well defined); Ward then gets simulation verification rather than proof. That is a respectable division of labour for a QF paper and I would not attempt more.

**The metric for ΔQ.** Var(ΔQ) needs ΔQ defined on the partition lattice. For theory, Meilă's variation of information is the right object (a true metric with clean decompositions); for practice we already use per-asset reassignment churn. State both, prove for one, report both empirically.

### 3.3 The objective min_P Var(ΔQ): fix the degeneracy first

As written the objective is degenerate: Var(ΔQ) is minimised by any *constant* partition — δ → ∞, one giant cluster, or a frozen map all achieve zero. This is not a technicality; it is the known central caveat of the stability literature (stability alone does not select good clusterings — [Ben-David et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Sober-Look-at-Clustering-Stability-Ben-David-Luxburg/95934f766b45e50076015a4283b5e2d4109312b9)), and it is exactly why the S1–S8 sweeps carried ARI and resolution guards, and why δ = 0.20 was rejected as over-frozen. The well-posed programme is one of:

- **Constrained:** min_P E[churn] subject to fidelity — recovery of the population partition with probability ≥ 1−α (theory), or ARI-to-baseline within a band (practice, which is what the sweeps already do); or
- **Lagrangian:** min_P E[churn] + γ·distortion, which is precisely the evolutionary-clustering objective (snapshot cost + temporal cost) of Chakrabarti–Kumar–Tomkins, putting our M1/M2 in their frame with the weight now *derived* from the noise floor instead of tuned.

So the note's optimisation idea is feasible and already half-implemented empirically; the theoretical contribution is replacing the swept guard with a derived constraint. Framed this way, the whole of section 3 becomes one sentence: **choose the clustering parameters so that partition changes require statistical evidence exceeding the estimator's own noise floor** — the same design principle as the Paper A gate, now with a proof sketch behind it.

### 3.4 What I would not claim

No general asymptotic theory of ΔQ for arbitrary linkages and unseparated data (out of reach, and Carlsson–Mémoli says Ward lacks the stability axioms such a theory would need). No stationarity "theorem" dressed up as deep (it is a lemma). No claim that stability selects the true clustering (it does not; the constraint does the selecting). And keep IGARCH as a stated boundary case, not a workhorse.

## 4. Where this lands in the QF paper, and scope control

This framework is the theoretical backbone the roadmap review's P1 stage anticipated, and it strengthens the paper's central claim rather than competing with it. Concretely I would put into the paper: the stationarity lemma (three lines, appendix), the conditional law of ΔΣ with the GARCH kurtosis multiplier (half page, feeding the calibrated δ), the boundary-flip proposition for the flat cut under a G-block model with the hysteresis corollary (the crux of the methods section), and the two testable predictions (δ scaling across universes' spans and kurtosis; churn scaling with rebalancing frequency) verified in the empirical section. The full phase-diagram characterisation of the stationary law (concentration thresholds in (separation, d/N), detoned vs raw) is a second paper — plausibly the follow-up home for the residual-clustering arm if it outgrows its timebox — and stapling it into the QF manuscript would break the one-central-claim rule.

**Amendments to the proposed roadmap** (extends P1; no change to the stage order): P1a — write the lemma, the ΔΣ law, and the boundary-flip proposition with the δ corollary. P1b — simulation validation on synthetic G-block panels with GARCH volatilities: measured churn vs the Φ-formula across (separation, N, δ, Θ), Ward included empirically. P1c — empirical margin-distribution exhibit from the existing 60-snapshot S&P 500 panel (the histogram of assignment margins in units of σ_d is the picture that explains the churn floor). P1d — deep-read list before any novelty claim: von Luxburg 2010, Ben-David et al. 2006, Bunea et al. 2020 (CORD/PECOK), Carlsson–Mémoli 2010, Nelson–Foster 1992/1994, Pafka–Potters–Kondor 2004, Meilă (variation of information), Marti et al. 2016.

**Rulings needed:** (1) adopt the constrained formulation (churn minimisation under a fidelity constraint) as the paper's stated objective; (2) scope: propositions-plus-simulation inside the QF paper, phase-diagram theory deferred to a follow-up; (3) whether P1a–P1c enter the current P1 stage or run as a parallel theory track.
