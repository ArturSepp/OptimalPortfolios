## Outcome

The 30% Equity / 30% Commodities / 30% Fixed Income / 10% FX budget modestly
improves all three constrained long-only portfolios relative to the accepted equal
25/25/25/25 budget. The gain is small: 6.51--13.62 bp/year at q=20% and
7.52--14.24 bp/year at q=25%. Sharpe rises by 0.0209--0.0222 at q=20% and
0.0237--0.0269 at q=25%.

Under the primary q=20% construction, M1-star has the strongest risk-adjusted result:

- 2.0416% net annual return;
- 4.8810% annualised volatility;
- 0.4395 RF=0 Sharpe;
- 0.9505 annualised one-way turnover;
- 38.68 bp/year cost drag.

Against the fair 30/30/30/10 global-within-sleeve rank, M1-star gives up 15.89 bp/year
of net return but reduces volatility by 1.0709 percentage points and raises Sharpe by
0.0430. It also reduces turnover by 0.1814 and cost drag by 7.42 bp/year. Baseline
clustering gives up 22.20 bp/year and raises Sharpe by 0.0284.

The q=25% robustness gives the same verdict: M1-star trails the same-budget global rank
by 12.86 bp/year, while improving Sharpe by 0.0415. Therefore clustering **beats global
on risk-adjusted performance, volatility, and turnover, but not on absolute return**.

The unconstrained original global rank remains the highest-return long-only portfolio
at 3.0639% net/year, but with 8.6470% volatility. M1-star's Sharpe is 0.0460 higher than
that original global leg despite 102.24 bp/year lower net return. This comparison is
informative but is not the clean clustering test because the strategic exposures differ.

Long-short remains unattractive. Both cluster legs have negative net return and Sharpe
at q=20% and q=25%; neither beats the same-budget global spread. The 30/30/30/10 budget
does not change the accepted rejection of the futures cluster long-short payoff.

## Frozen design

No covariance or clustering refit was performed. The accepted baseline and M1-star
caches were consumed unchanged.

| component | value |
|---|---|
| universe | 95 global futures contracts |
| strategic budget | 30% Equity / 30% Commodities / 30% Fixed Income / 10% FX |
| signal | 48-week production momentum, latest four weeks skipped |
| primary selection | q = 0.20 |
| robustness | q = 0.25 |
| decisions | ME, 2002-01-31 through 2026-07-31, 295 dates |
| performance returns | W-WED, simple non-excess NAV returns |
| implementation lag | one W-WED observation |
| costs | 20 bp |
| cluster treatments | baseline and calibrated M1-star only |
| long-only exposure | +1 |
| long-short exposure | +1 / -1, gross 2, net 0 |

The target is imposed identically on the global-within-sleeve control and cluster legs.
Within each sleeve the global control ranks contracts together. Cluster treatments rank
within correlation clusters split by broad sleeve, give available clusters equal budgets,
and split each cluster budget equally among selected contracts. For long-short portfolios,
30/30/30/10 is imposed independently on both signed sides.

EW-all remains reference-only for beta and alpha calculations. It is not a ranking leg,
performance yardstick, or acceptance comparison.

## Budget sensitivity against 25/25/25/25

This comparison changes only the strategic sleeve weights. Signal, selection, cluster
cache, lag, costs, and backtest engine are identical.

|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.20 | global-within-sleeve | +13.62 bp | +0.0309 pp | +0.0209 | +0.0067 |
| 0.20 | baseline cluster | +6.51 bp | -0.1251 pp | +0.0222 | +0.0491 |
| 0.20 | M1-star cluster | +8.87 bp | -0.0552 pp | +0.0222 | +0.0608 |
| 0.25 | global-within-sleeve | +14.24 bp | -0.0413 pp | +0.0269 | -0.0066 |

The bundle of three five-point increases to Equity, Commodities, and Fixed Income funded
by a fifteen-point reduction in FX is directionally helpful for long-only performance.
However, the absolute improvement is only about 7--14 bp/year. This is an exploratory
budget sensitivity, not evidence that 30/30/30/10 is an optimised or statistically
superior allocation. Any budget selection claim would require a frozen grid and an
out-of-sample or split-sample decision rule.
