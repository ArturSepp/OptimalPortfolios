\begin{abstract}
	\noindent Capital market assumptions (CMAs) are the primary quantitative input to strategic asset allocation, yet allocation committees adopt provider numbers they cannot audit, and the optimizer prices every unattributed return as alpha. We propose an audit in which any provider's CMA vector decomposes, against the committee's own factor risk model, into implied factor premia plus an unattributed return, and we develop the MATF-CMA framework, in which every deviation from the factor span is a declared and governed channel.

	\noindent We organize the empirical analysis around three committee decisions on a production 18-asset universe. The provider choice: on a common risk model, Balanced-mandate expected returns run from $8.3\%$ to $11.3\%$ across four anonymised providers and the 41-advisor survey consensus, on nearly identical portfolios; the audit attributes each book to implied premia and unattributed return, and the consensus is nearly factor-consistent while the production alternatives assumptions claim more idiosyncratic Sharpe content than the entire factor opportunity set. The alpha admission: production admissions claim an idiosyncratic squared Sharpe ratio of $1.44$ against a factor opportunity set of $0.53$, and three caps---two per sleeve, one portfolio budget---turn the policy into pass-fail audit tests.

	The scenario choice: one vector of eleven factor shocks reprices every asset, admitted alpha does not shock, and a one-sigma floor per mandate bounds the approved stress case. A bootstrap of 25 years of returns shows the factor structure cuts the frontier's sampling bandwidth by more than half, equivalent to five times the sample.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Three Key Takeaways:}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
	\item With the risk model, the benchmark, and the constraints held fixed, the CMA provider choice moves the Balanced-mandate expected return from $8.3\%$ (MATF) to $11.3\%$ (the most aggressive of four anonymised providers). The MATF-CMA framework gives the Investment Committee an auditable basis for that choice: any provider's CMA vector, and its own, decomposes into implied factor premia plus an unattributed return against the committee's risk model.
	\item How much of the alternatives' historical excess return to admit as alpha is the single most consequential CMA decision. Scaling the admission from zero to the production policy lifts the claimed portfolio Sharpe ratio from $0.33$ to $0.39$ with almost no change in weights: mandate guardrails contain the allocation, and only the admission policy contains the claim. We propose three auditable caps for governing it, including a portfolio-level budget that prices the committee's diversification preference sleeve by sleeve.
	\item Consistency and uncertainty are quantified rather than asserted. The framework's deviation from the factor span reconciles exactly to its declared adjustments, against unattributable residuals of up to $5.6\%$ per asset under per-asset construction, and the factor structure with an economic prior halves the sampling bandwidth of the efficient frontier.
\end{itemize}

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{Capital Market Assumptions and Strategic Asset Allocation Using Multi-Asset Tradable Factors}\label{sec:intro}

Capital market assumptions (CMAs) are long-term estimates of expected return, risk, and correlation for investable asset classes, and they are the primary quantitative input to strategic asset allocation (SAA). An allocation committee that signs off a CMA set is implicitly taking three decisions. It chooses whose expected returns to believe, it decides how much of the alternatives' historical excess return to treat as repeatable alpha, and it approves the scenarios under which the allocation will be stressed. The dominant production methodologies support none of these decisions auditably.

The dominant methodologies build CMAs asset by asset. Grinold-Kroner-style decompositions \citep{GrinoldKroner2002} sum per-asset income, growth, and valuation components, and qualitative frameworks map assets to factor scores that are set by hand. Both approaches produce expected returns with no structural link to the covariance model used in the optimization, so the return vector generically lies outside the span of the risk model's factor loadings. The unattributed part is not harmless. An unattributed residual is observationally an undeclared factor, and the optimizer prices it and harvests it like one \citep{CampbellLoMacKinlay1997}. We measure both halves of this failure on a production 18-asset universe. Per-asset CMAs built from historical means leave unattributed residuals with per-asset medians between $5$ and $180$ basis points and a portfolio-level norm with a median of $4.2\%$ across bootstrap draws. Point estimates reach $5.6\%$ on single assets. No diagnostic can trace these residuals to an intended assumption. A second weakness compounds the first. Per-asset point estimates inherit the noise of correlated component forecasts, and we show by bootstrap that the resulting frontier bandwidth is roughly twice what a factor structure with an economic prior delivers.

Exhibit~\ref{fig:unintended_exposures} shows the allocation consequence. We solve a long-only allocation at the benchmark volatility of $9.4\%$ twice, on the identical risk model, once under the MATF-CMAs of this paper and once under per-asset historical means. The historical-mean book claims a materially higher ex-ante Sharpe ratio on broad unapproved factor tilts and carries the majority of its active risk in idiosyncratic residuals. Under the constraints of a real mandate the two books converge in weights and still diverge in the claim. Unaudited CMAs damage the allocation where governance is light and damage the communicated numbers everywhere.

The audit that supports all three committee decisions is one operator. Given the loading matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ and residual variances $\boldsymbol D$ of the committee's own risk model, any excess-CMA vector $\boldsymbol\mu_e$---the committee's own or any provider's---decomposes uniquely into an implied factor-premium vector and an orthogonal unattributed return, whose size in squared-Sharpe units is the claimed idiosyncratic content of the vector. The decomposition, its statistical tests, and its use for universe selection are developed in the companion paper \citep{SeppKastenholzFAJ2026}; this paper consumes the measure and puts it to committee work: it prices the provider choice, it bounds the alpha admission, and it verifies the framework's own construction. Appendix~A states the operator.

This paper develops the MATF-CMA framework, which makes the three committee decisions explicit and auditable. The framework rests on two objects. The factor risk premia $\boldsymbol\lambda = (\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_M)^{\intercal}$ are expected excess returns of $M=11$ tradable factor portfolios, estimated from market-implied decompositions for the fundamental factors and from equilibrium Sharpe ratios for the structural factors. The factor loadings $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_i$ measure each asset's sensitivity to the factors, estimated by a sign-constrained clustered regression.\footnote{Throughout, bold lowercase Greek denotes column vectors (e.g., $\boldsymbol\lambda$, $\boldsymbol\alpha$) and bold uppercase Greek or Roman denotes matrices (e.g., $\Sigma$, $\boldsymbol\beta$); non-bold letters are scalars and $\intercal$ is the transpose.} The asset-level CMA is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cma_i}
	\text{CMA}_i = r_f^{ref,3Y} + \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_i^{\intercal} \boldsymbol\lambda + \text{declared adjustments}_i,
\end{equation}
where the declared adjustments of Section ``The MATF-CMA Construction'' are logged per asset and default to zero. The same loading matrix defines the risk model,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cma_ii}
	\Sigma = \boldsymbol\beta\, \Sigma_F\, \boldsymbol\beta^{\intercal} + D,
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma_F$ the $M \times M$ factor covariance matrix and $D$ the diagonal matrix of residual variances. Because Equations~\eqref{eq:cma_i} and~\eqref{eq:cma_ii} share $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$, the CMAs are alpha-beta consistent up to the declared adjustments, and the reconciliation between the two is exact rather than approximate.

The structural guarantee distinguishes MATF-CMA from three related constructions. \cite{CeriaStubbsAlpha2013} achieve consistency procedurally through multi-stage factor-mimicking portfolios. \cite{BlackLitterman1992} guarantee spanning by reverse-optimizing from market-capitalization weights, which anchors expected returns to cap weights rather than to market observables. \cite{RoncalliXu2024} prove a zero residual premium for the tangency portfolio in a factor reformulation, again with cap-weight-derived premia. MATF-CMA obtains the guarantee by construction from market-observable premia, and it extends the guarantee to a governed exception: deviations from the factor span are permitted only through declared channels, so the committee sees every exception it grants. The framework complements the ROSAA tactical allocation framework of \citet{Rosaa2026}, which optimizes multi-fund active portfolios against a risk-budgeted SAA benchmark; MATF-CMA constructs the long-horizon strategic $\boldsymbol\mu$ that anchors that benchmark, so the two layers stack.

The paper is organized around the three decisions, audit first. The next section prices and attributes the provider choice. Section ``The MATF-CMA Construction'' presents the framework at the level the committee signs off, with the premium estimation in Appendix~C and the loading estimation cited to the companion methodology papers. Section ``Decision Two'' prices the alpha admission and proposes three governance caps. Section ``Decision Three'' defines scenarios as factor-shock vectors with a stress floor per mandate. Section ``From Dials to Weights'' traces the committee's settings into the Balanced mandate. Section ``How Much to Trust the Numbers'' quantifies estimation uncertainty and verifies the consistency reconciliation, and the final section summarizes the operational consequences.

\section*{Decision One: Whose Numbers to Believe --- Five Providers and the Consensus, Audited}\label{sec:provider}

Allocation committees choose a CMA provider before they choose an allocation, and the choice is rarely priced. We price it directly, and then we attribute it. We optimize the same mandate, on the same benchmark, under the same constraints, on the same risk model, and change only the expected returns: the MATF-CMAs of this paper against the published sets of four institutional providers, anonymised as A through D, and the 41-advisor survey consensus of \citet{HorizonSurvey2025}.\footnote{All provider returns are arithmetic total returns for a USD investor. Native-currency lines convert by uncovered interest parity with each provider's own cash rates. Sleeves without a published provider line are held at the MATF value and carry no cross-provider tilt by construction. The consensus column maps the survey's published 10-year arithmetic averages to the paper universe; the insurance-linked sleeve has no survey line and is held at MATF. The provider anonymisation map is maintained by the authors outside the replication package.} Holding the risk model fixed is a deliberate simplification: it isolates the return disagreement and understates any disagreement in the providers' risk views, and we accept the trade-off because asset-level provider covariances are not published in comparable form.

Exhibit~\ref{tab:provider_saa} reports the Balanced-with-Alternatives solution per provider. The headline is the top row of Panel~B. On an identical risk model with an identical $10.5$--$10.7\%$ volatility book, the expected total return runs from $8.3\%$ under MATF to $11.3\%$ under Provider~B, with the survey consensus at $8.4\%$, and the implied excess Sharpe ratio runs from $0.39$ to $0.67$. Nothing in the portfolios justifies the spread, because the portfolios are nearly the same book. The spread is a pure disagreement about expected returns, and the committee that adopts a provider adopts it.

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{The MATF-CMA Construction: Assumptions That Pass Their Own Audit}\label{sec:framework}

This section presents the framework at the level the Investment Committee signs off: eleven factors with their premia, one loading matrix shared with the risk model, and four declared adjustment channels. The premium estimation sits in Appendix~C; the loading estimator is developed in the companion methodology papers \citep{SeppFactorLassoCSDA2026,SeppFactorLassoJSS2026}.

\subsection*{Eleven Tradable Factors and Blended Premia}\label{subsec:factors}

The MATF specification uses eleven tradable factors, each implemented as an investable, volatility-targeted portfolio of futures or index trackers.\footnote{% custom-recut generated: lineage footnote
We apply the MATF model of \cite{Rosaa2026} with four changes: we add a rates-volatility factor, a risk-only FX factor, and an emerging-market credit factor, and we replace the single carry factor with investable G10 and EM currency-carry indices. The eleven-factor selection was adopted after measured comparisons against the nine-factor base model and a regional-equity variant, documented in the replication repository. Volatility targets are differentiated by asset class: $15\%$ for Equity and Commodities, $7\%$ for Private Equity and FX, $6\%$ for Rates, and $5\%$ for the remaining factors. Rescaling a factor rescales its betas inversely and leaves CMAs, covariances, and optimization outputs unchanged.} Tradability is the discipline of the framework: every premium is the expected excess return of a portfolio the desk could hold, not a score.

Exhibit~\ref{tab:nine_factors} lists the factors, the source of each premium, and the current values on the production cut. The premia divide into two groups. The fundamental factors carry market-implied premia: the Equity premium from the payout- and cyclically-adjusted earnings yield (P-CAEY) of \citet{HaghaniWhite2024}, the Rates premium from the OIS-implied term premium plus curve roll-down, and the Credit and Credit EM premia from CDS and sovereign spreads net of actuarial expected loss \citep{Ilmanen2011}. Each fundamental premium is blended $50/50$ between its current market-implied value and its long-run equilibrium anchor; the blend weight is a declared control of the framework, not an estimation artifact, and changing it reprices every CMA visibly. The structural factors, for which no market yield provides a clean decomposition, carry equilibrium premia set as a long-run Sharpe ratio times the factor's volatility target. The rates-volatility factor prices the short-volatility exposure embedded in corporate assets \citep{Morris2026}, and the FX factor carries a zero premium by an equilibrium argument: the dollar level is uncompensated, while the compensated currency premium is already in the carry factors \citep{LustigRV2011}.

\subsection*{One Loading Matrix and Four Declared Channels}\label{subsec:loadings}

The factor loadings $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ are estimated once per production cut from a regression of asset excess returns on factor excess returns, with two economic disciplines. First, loadings carry sign constraints that encode economic priors, summarized in Exhibit~\ref{tab:sign_constraints}: core risk premia enter non-negatively for long-only assets, the rates-volatility loading is non-positive because corporate assets are implicitly short rates volatility, and the Private Equity factor is reserved for private-market assets. Second, a clustering-based regularization stabilizes the loadings of correlated factors and correlated assets. The estimator, its statistical properties, and the production software are developed in the companion methodology papers \citep{SeppFactorLassoCSDA2026,SeppFactorLassoJSS2026}; the same matrix $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ defines the covariance model of Equation~\eqref{eq:cma_ii} and anchors the audit of Section ``Decision One''.

The framework operates natively in excess return space. The factor-implied expected excess return of asset $i$ in reference currency $ref$ is $\text{CMA}_i^{(ref),factor} = \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_i^{(ref)\intercal} \boldsymbol\lambda$, and the published excess CMA adds four declared adjustment channels:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cma2a}
	\text{CMA}_i^{(ref),excess} = \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_i^{(ref)\intercal} \boldsymbol\lambda + b_i + w_i \cdot \alpha_i^{(ref)} + c_i + a_i .
\end{equation}
The regional valuation blend $b_i$ applies to benchmark equity and government sleeves, where the regional market-implied premium of Appendix~C differs from the global factor-implied value. The admitted historical alpha $w_i \cdot \alpha_i$ applies to selected alternatives, where $\alpha_i$ is the exponentially weighted mean of the loading-regression residuals, estimated on the same spans as the loadings, and $w_i \in [0,1]$ is the admission weight set by the committee and governed in Section ``Decision Two''. The discretionary add-on $c_i$ carries any remaining committee adjustment as a logged per-asset entry; on the current cut its only non-zero use is a $-200$ basis-point adjustment to the emerging-market regional earnings yield, declared in the workbook.

The fee add-on $a_i$ converts selected fund CMAs to a gross-of-fee basis where required for reporting and is zero on this universe. Equities and bonds carry $w_i = 0$, so their consistency with the factor model is exact up to the blend. The total CMA adds the reference-currency risk-free rate per Equation~\eqref{eq:cma_i}, where the 3-year government yield aligns with the CMA horizon and carries minimal term-premium contamination \citep{ACM2013}. The multi-currency construction holds $\boldsymbol\lambda$ identical across reference currencies and re-estimates loadings against reference-currency returns (Appendix~D).

The four components sum to the published number exactly, and the replication code asserts the identity on the workbook at each production cut. The waterfall is the audit artifact of the framework, and it is the framework's own row in the provider audit of Section ``Decision One'': the committee that approves it has seen every deviation from the factor model it is granting, priced in the same units it applies to external providers.

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{Decision Two: The Alpha Admission, Governed}\label{sec:admission}

Alternatives carry the largest CMA disagreements and the strongest external pressure. The provider comparison quantifies the pressure: matching the mainstream providers on the Balanced mandate requires roughly $50$ to $75$ basis points of additional expected return, and consultants translate such gaps into requests for higher alternatives assumptions. The MATF-CMA framework accepts such requests through exactly one channel: the Investment Committee sets the admission weight $w_i \in [0,1]$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:cma2a}, and the published workbook logs the admitted alpha $w_i \alpha_i$ for every asset. Under per-asset construction the same request disappears into adjustments that no diagnostic can attribute. This section prices the dial: what the admission does to the allocation, what it does to the reported numbers, and which controls keep it defensible.

\subsection*{The Admission Dial and What the Guardrails Prevent}\label{subsec:admission_dial}

We scale the admitted alpha of every sleeve by a common factor $s$ and re-solve the Balanced mandate at each value: $s=0$ is the pure factor book, $s=1$ the production policy, and $s>1$ the direction of consultant pressure, under the constraints of the SAA application (weights within $\pm 50\%$ of the benchmark, tracking error capped at $1.5\%$).

Exhibit~\ref{fig:admission_dial} reports the sweep. At $s=0$ the mandate claims an ex-ante excess Sharpe ratio of $0.33$. The production admission lifts the claim to $0.39$, and scaling one half beyond production lifts it to $0.43$. The optimal weights barely move, because the box and the tracking-error cap hold total alternatives near $37\%$ at every scale. Private Equity sits at its $22.5\%$ box cap throughout, so its admission changes no allocation and only raises the reported number. The composition rotates underneath: Private Credit moves from cap to floor while Insurance-Linked and Gold move from floor to cap. The conclusion is uncomfortable and precise: the mandate guardrails contain the portfolio, and nothing in the optimization contains the claim.

\subsection*{What the Admission Buys}\label{subsec:admission_sr2}

Admission weights claim idiosyncratic Sharpe content, and the claim can be priced with the same audit that priced the providers. On the frozen production cut, the eleven factor premia offer a frictionless squared Sharpe ratio of $0.53$, of which the 18-asset universe makes $0.21$ attainable. The raw production admissions claim an idiosyncratic squared Sharpe ratio of $1.44$, more than twice the entire factor opportunity set. The generalized-least-squares projection of \citet{SeppKastenholzFAJ2026} strips the component of admitted alpha that is factor premium in disguise, and the honest claim drops to $0.48$. The projection also attributes the disguise per sleeve: $97\%$ of the Private Equity admission and $96\%$ of the Private Credit admission are premium-like, against $79\%$ for Insurance-Linked, $37\%$ for Hedge Funds, and $26\%$ for Gold. We read the Private Equity share as independent confirmation of appraisal smoothing, because the cross-sectional projection reaches the same verdict as cash-flow-based loading estimates by a different route. The Insurance-Linked share is the limit case worth stating plainly: with $R^2 = 0.16$ against the factor set, the sleeve is held almost entirely on non-factor grounds, and yet $79\%$ of its admitted alpha is statistically indistinguishable from factor premium.

Even the honest $0.48$ exceeds everything the factor set can deliver through these assets. A committee that signs the production admissions therefore asserts more skill content than systematic content in the book. The assertion may be right. It should at least be visible, and the next subsection makes it testable.

\subsection*{Three Auditable Caps and the Governed Dial}\label{subsec:admission_caps}

Visibility invites control. We propose three caps that turn the admission policy into pass-fail tests a validator can run from the published workbook. Cap~1 bounds the admitted information ratio, $w_i \alpha_i / \sigma_{\varepsilon,i} \le 0.50$, at the maximum factor Sharpe prior in the framework: no sleeve should claim more reward per unit of residual risk than the best-rewarded systematic factor. Cap~2 bounds the above-factor share of the excess CMA at $50\%$, so that factor economics remain the primary driver of every published assumption. The two sleeve caps target distinct failures---over-claimed skill and crowded-out factor economics---but neither bounds the total: under the diagonal residual matrix the claims aggregate as $SR^2_{\alpha} = \sum_i (w_i \alpha_i / \sigma_{\varepsilon,i})^2$, so six sleeves at the Cap~1 bound would claim $1.5$ with every sleeve individually compliant. Cap~3 closes the gap at the portfolio level:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap3}
	SR^2_{\alpha} \;\le\; \kappa \cdot SR^2_{MATF},
\end{equation}
the committee may not claim more squared Sharpe from admitted skill than the factor opportunity set delivers through the universe it actually holds, scaled by the policy parameter $\kappa \le 1$. The bound is economic rather than statistical: risk-based deviations from factor pricing are bounded in Sharpe units while deviations produced to sell are not, so the size of the claim is itself the diagnostic \citep{SeppKastenholzFAJ2026}. Keeping the residual matrix diagonal while admitting large alpha asserts the non-risk case by construction, and bounding the aggregate claim is the consistent resolution.

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{Decision Three: Coherent Scenarios, Now with Floors}\label{sec:scenarios}

Strategic asset allocation requires stress and upside cases in addition to the base CMAs, and the committee must decide in which language to approve them. Defining scenarios asset by asset is ad hoc and risks internal inconsistency: a stress table with equities down, credit unchanged, and hedge funds flat encodes cross-asset claims that nobody has checked. We instead define every scenario as one vector of factor shocks and let the loading matrix reprice all assets, which guarantees that factor co-movements are empirically consistent because the shocks are realized returns from historical episodes.

Historical factor returns are annual realizations, while the CMAs operate on a 5--10 year horizon, so we de-compound the annual factor return over the CMA horizon, $\Delta f_k^{5Y} = f_k^{annual,scenario}/T$ with $T=5$. The scenario CMA for asset $i$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cma_scenario}
	\text{CMA}_i^{scenario} = \text{CMA}_i^{base} + \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_i^{\intercal} \Delta \boldsymbol f^{5Y},
\end{equation}
additive on the base CMA, so declared adjustments carry into every scenario unchanged; the replication code asserts the additive identity on the published workbook at each production cut. We select 2022 as the stress scenario, with a simultaneous equity and rates sell-off, and 2023 as the upside scenario, with an equity and credit recovery. Exhibit~\ref{tab:factor_returns} reports the annual factor returns and the de-compounded bumps: the committee approves eleven numbers per scenario, and every asset-level and mandate-level consequence follows mechanically.

\paragraph*{Scenario Floors.} The approved stress case also carries a bound. For every mandate we impose one linear constraint per approved episode: the stress-repriced expected total return of the mandate book may not fall below the benchmark expectation minus one long-term benchmark volatility,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
	\text{CMA}^{stress}_{book} \;\ge\; \mu_{bm} - n \cdot \sigma_{bm}, \qquad n = 1,
\end{equation}
with $\mu_{bm}$ the benchmark expected total return under the base CMAs, $\sigma_{bm}$ the benchmark volatility from the risk model, and $n$ the declared policy multiplier. The rule is scalable---it requires no hand-set number per mandate---and it is invariant to the reference cash rate, which cancels on both sides. On the production cut the floor is slack on all eight mandates: the Balanced floor sits at $-1.6\%$ against a stress-repriced $4.8\%$, so on the approved 2022 episode Cap~3, not the floor, is the operative constraint on the admission policy. The floor earns its place as the committee's backstop for harsher episodes and more aggressive admission requests, and its interaction with the caps is traced in the governed dial of Appendix~E.

The implication for governance mirrors the previous section. The scenario debate reduces to eleven factor shocks per episode plus one floor multiplier, approved once and applied everywhere, and the stress report separates what markets do to the book from what the committee has assumed about skill. Under per-asset construction both statements are unavailable, because the scenario table and the alpha assumptions are fused inside the same hand-set numbers.

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{How Much to Trust the Numbers}\label{sec:trust}

The decisions of the previous sections consume the CMAs as given. The committee's remaining question is calibration: how much of the expected performance is signal, and how much is estimation noise inherited from the inputs? The stake is practical. Under per-asset sample-mean CMAs, the policy benchmark itself sits inside the $90\%$ confidence band of the optimized frontier, so a CIO cannot statistically distinguish the proposed SAA from the benchmark on expected return alone. This section reports two audits: the sampling uncertainty of the frontier and the reconciliation of the consistency residual. Appendix~B develops the bootstrap design.

\subsection*{Estimation Uncertainty: The Frontier Bandwidth}\label{subsec:trust_bootstrap}

The three CMA constructions carry very different sampling noise on the same data. The per-asset sample mean over an effective $25$ years of monthly returns has a standard error of $3.2\%$ for US equity. A Grinold-Kroner building-block construction is no better at $3.8\%$, because each component is a forecast of a separate process and the variance does not shrink with the sample. The MATF construction estimates $M=11$ factor Sharpe ratios jointly across the whole universe under an economic prior, and the same US-equity standard error falls to $1.5\%$. The prior dispersion of $\sigma_{SR}=0.10$ is empirically grounded: \citet{Lemperiere2017} document that long-run Sharpe ratios of well-identified risk premia cluster in a narrow band around $0.30$. The advantage is structural: the framework estimates fewer parameters using more data. Appendix~B derives the three standard errors and reports the $\sigma_{SR}$ sensitivity grid.

Exhibit~\ref{fig:bootstrap_frontier} shows the consequence for the object the committee actually approves. Across $B=500$ stationary block-bootstrap draws of a $300$-month window of multi-asset returns, the $90\%$ bandwidth of the long-only frontier under the MATF estimator is well under half of the per-asset bandwidth at the mandate volatility target, a reduction of $2.3\times$ that is stable across the volatility grid. The information gain is equivalent to roughly $5\times$ the historical sample, since sample-mean variance scales inversely with the sample size. The equivalence explains why per-asset SAA fails at realistic samples: \citet{DeMiguelGarlappiUppal2009} estimate that sample-mean mean-variance needs around $3{,}000$ months to beat equal weighting on $25$ assets, roughly ten times the record that exists. Forward-looking per-asset views shift the point estimate and not the uncertainty around it, so the bandwidth comparison extends to Grinold-Kroner constructions.

\subsection*{Consistency: Declared versus Unattributed Deviation}\label{subsec:trust_consistency}

The second audit concerns the consistency residual itself. For any excess-CMA vector and any factor risk model, the residual $\boldsymbol\Delta$ is the component the factor span cannot explain (Appendix~A); the diagnostic is model-independent, which makes it the natural companion to the provider comparison of Section ``Decision One''. Under the MATF construction the residual is not zero, and the framework does not claim it is: the residual equals the annihilated image of the declared channels, so the claim is exact reconciliation---every basis point of $\boldsymbol\Delta$ traces to a logged entry in the construction waterfall of Exhibit~\ref{fig:construction_waterfall}, and the replication code asserts the reconciliation on the published workbook at each production cut. On the frozen cut the maximum reconciliation gap is of the order $10^{-16}$, which is numerical zero.

Per-asset constructions offer no such reconciliation. Exhibit~\ref{fig:declared_delta} compares the two on the frozen universe with identical loadings and residual variances. The historical-mean construction leaves unattributed residuals of up to $5.6\%$ per asset with a median near $0.6\%$, concentrated in the low-$R^2$ alternatives and the single-country equity sleeves; across bootstrap draws the per-asset medians run from $5$ to $180$ basis points and the portfolio-level violation norm has a median of $4.2\%$ (Appendix~B). The practical reading is the one from the opening section: $35$ basis points of median unattributed residual on the insurance-linked sleeve is $35$ basis points the optimizer prices as alpha, and the alpha has no replication strategy behind it. The framework does not promise zero deviation. It promises zero unattributed deviation, and the promise is checked by machine rather than by assertion.

The two audits give the committee its calibration in two numbers. The expected-return surface it approves carries less than half the sampling bandwidth of a per-asset construction on the same record, and every deviation of that surface from the factor model reconciles exactly to a decision the committee itself has made.

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{Implications for Practice}\label{sec:practice}

The framework changes what an Investment Committee debates. Under per-asset construction, CMA review is a negotiation over dozens of correlated component forecasts, with cross-asset consistency maintained informally and stress cases specified asset by asset. Under MATF-CMA, the review reduces to a small set of controls that are explicit, separated, and auditable from the published workbook.

\paragraph*{The Control Inventory.} Practitioner judgment concentrates in eight controls. The eleven factor Sharpe ratios set the premia of Exhibit~\ref{tab:nine_factors}, and the blend weight sets how far the fundamental premia lean toward current market pricing. The prior dispersion $\sigma_{SR}$ sets how tightly the premia anchor to their long-run levels in the uncertainty analysis. The sign constraints of Exhibit~\ref{tab:sign_constraints} encode the economic priors of the loading matrix. The four declared adjustment channels of Equation~\eqref{eq:cma2a} govern every deviation from the factor model, with the admission weights $w_i \in [0,1]$ bounded by the three caps of Exhibits~\ref{tab:admission_audit} and~\ref{tab:cap3_grid}. The scenario vectors define stress and upside cases in eleven numbers each, and the floor multiplier $n$ backstops the approved stress case per mandate. Every other object, including loadings, residual variances, and asset-level CMAs, is mechanical. The controls close an audit loop: the waterfall shows what was decided, the caps test whether the admissions stay within policy, and the reconciliation of Section ``How Much to Trust the Numbers'' verifies that nothing else entered.

\paragraph*{Cross-Currency Deployment.} Institutions running mandates in several base currencies typically maintain parallel CMA systems with separate inflation and FX forecasts per region. The excess-return construction removes the duplication: all currency panels share the same factor premia, no explicit inflation forecast is required because regional inflation is embedded in the local risk-free rate, and currency-specific inputs reduce to the local rate curve and the hedging basis. The invariance extends to the optimization itself: because the mandate solve maximizes active return under a budget constraint, the reference cash anchor cancels identically, so one optimization serves every operating currency and hedged mandates hold the same book by construction (Appendix~D).

\paragraph*{Migrating an Existing Process.} The consistency diagnostic of Appendix~A is the natural entry point: applied to the incumbent CMA vector and risk model, it returns the baseline magnitude of unattributed deviation in the current optimization, directly comparable to the $4.2\%$ median norm measured for the sample-mean construction. Three migration paths then cover the common methodologies. A Grinold-Kroner process replaces four component forecasts per asset with eleven factor Sharpe ratios and estimated loadings. A qualitative factor mapping replaces hand-set factor weights with estimated loadings while keeping the committee's debate over factor views. A Black-Litterman process replaces the market-cap equilibrium prior with $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\boldsymbol\lambda$ and restates views in factor space, which removes the view-uncertainty matrix as a calibration object \citep{RoncalliXu2024}. Migration need not be atomic: an institution can adopt the factor structure and loadings first, retain its premium views, and shift the premium calibration to the market-implied channels as confidence grows.

\sectionfont{\MakeUppercase}
\section*{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We developed the MATF-CMA framework, in which every asset's expected return is its loading on eleven tradable factors times market-implied factor premia, plus four declared adjustment channels logged per asset. The same loading matrix defines the covariance model, so consistency between expected returns and risk is a reconciliation the replication code verifies, not a property the practitioner asserts, and the same operator audits any external provider's numbers against the committee's own risk model. We organized the empirical analysis around the three decisions an allocation committee takes when it adopts a CMA set, on a production 18-asset universe at the 2026-Q2 cut.

The quantified results are as follows. On the provider decision, four anonymised CMA sets and the 41-advisor consensus, optimized on a common risk model, benchmark, and constraints, produced Balanced-mandate expected returns from $8.3\%$ to $11.3\%$ on nearly identical portfolios; the audit attributed the spread, and the consensus proved nearly factor-consistent with a claimed idiosyncratic content of $0.10$ against $0.35$ for the production vector. On the admission decision, scaling the alternatives' admitted alpha from zero to the production policy lifted the claimed Sharpe ratio from $0.33$ to $0.39$ with almost no change in weights, and removing the mandate guardrails let the production admission place $48\%$ of the book in alternatives. The raw admissions claimed an idiosyncratic squared Sharpe of $1.44$ against a factor opportunity set of $0.53$, of which $0.48$ survived the premium projection of the companion paper; the three caps flagged the sleeves and the portfolio carrying the excess, and the Cap~3 grid priced the recuts per skill-share budget. On the scenario decision, one eleven-element shock vector repriced every asset and mandate, the Balanced stress case read an excess return of $+0.7\%$ at the production admission against a negative reading at zero admission because admitted alpha does not shock
% [TODO(sol): restore the exact zero-admission stress value once re-read.], and the one-sigma floor proved slack on every mandate, leaving Cap~3 as the operative constraint. On calibration, the factor structure with an economic prior cut the $90\%$ sampling bandwidth of the long-only frontier by $2.3\times$, equivalent to roughly five times the historical sample, and per-asset construction left unattributed residuals with per-asset medians of $5$ to $180$ basis points and a median portfolio norm of $4.2\%$, against exact reconciliation to the declared channels under MATF.

Two extensions lie outside this paper. The Sharpe-ratio accounting that prices admissions and universe selection in closed form, together with its exact statistical tests, is developed in the companion paper \citep{SeppKastenholzFAJ2026}. The private-equity extension, which replaces appraisal-based loading estimates with cash-flow-based ones and would shrink the statistical alpha the admission policy must govern, is deferred to future work.

\subsection*{Appendix A: The Consistency Audit}\label{app:consistency_diagnostic}

The audit used throughout the paper is the classical factor pricing-error apparatus \citep{Ross1976,Cochrane2005,CampbellLoMacKinlay1997}, repurposed as a forward-looking coherence check between a CMA vector and the loading matrix used in the same allocation problem. Given a loading matrix $\boldsymbol\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times M}$ of full column rank, the annihilator $\boldsymbol M_\beta = \boldsymbol I_N - \boldsymbol\beta(\boldsymbol\beta^\intercal\boldsymbol\beta)^{-1}\boldsymbol\beta^\intercal$ satisfies $\boldsymbol M_\beta\boldsymbol\beta = \boldsymbol 0$, and the consistency residual of any excess-CMA vector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consistency_residual}
	\boldsymbol\Delta(\boldsymbol\mu, \boldsymbol\beta) = \boldsymbol M_\beta\,(\boldsymbol\mu - r_f\boldsymbol 1),
\end{equation}
the component of the vector the factor span cannot explain. Under the MATF construction of Equation~\eqref{eq:cma2a}, $\boldsymbol\Delta = \boldsymbol M_{\hat\beta}(\boldsymbol b + \boldsymbol W\boldsymbol\alpha + \boldsymbol c + \boldsymbol a)$ with $\boldsymbol W = \text{diag}(w_1,\dots,w_N)$, $w_i \in [0,1]$: zero when no adjustments are declared, and otherwise the annihilated image of the declared channels, which is the exact-reconciliation property asserted by the replication code at each production cut. The diagnostic requires only the CMA vector and a loading matrix; for a proprietary risk model, the loadings can be taken as the leading principal components of its covariance, so the audit applies to any practitioner stack.

The economic size of a deviation is measured in the risk-adjusted metric the optimizer itself uses. The generalized-least-squares decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gls}
	\boldsymbol\lambda_{gls} = (\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^\intercal \boldsymbol D^{-1} \hat{\boldsymbol\beta})^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^\intercal \boldsymbol D^{-1}(\boldsymbol\mu - r_f\boldsymbol 1),
	\qquad
	SR^2_{\alpha}(\boldsymbol\mu) = \boldsymbol a^\intercal \boldsymbol D^{-1} \boldsymbol a, \quad \boldsymbol a = \boldsymbol\mu - r_f\boldsymbol 1 - \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\boldsymbol\lambda_{gls},
\end{equation}
splits any vector into implied factor premia and an orthogonal claim whose size is a squared Sharpe ratio, comparable across providers, universes, and dates. The derivation, the closed-form Sharpe accounting ($SR^2_{MATF}$, the frictionless ceiling, and the identification deficit between them), the premium-like share of a single admitted sleeve, and the exact finite-sample tests of the claim are developed in the companion paper \citep{SeppKastenholzFAJ2026}, with the classical lineage in \citet{Shanken1992} and \citet{CampbellLoMacKinlay1997}; this paper consumes the operators as decision tools.

\subsection*{Appendix B: Bootstrap Design and Estimation-Error Analysis}\label{app:bootstrap_design}

We compare two estimators of the long-only mean-variance efficient frontier on the 18-asset universe, comprising 14 monthly-frequency assets and 4 quarterly-frequency alternatives, over a $300$-month window from July 2001 to June 2026. The panel is used as recorded: two short-history sleeves (insurance-linked from September 2006, Europe ex-UK from November 2007) enter without pre-inception backfill, and all moment estimates are computed pairwise-complete over the observations each sleeve has. The full procedure is documented in the replication repository \citep{MatfCmaCode2026}.

\textbf{1) Raw asset-level estimator:} each draw computes the sample mean and pairwise-complete covariance of resampled asset returns and solves the long-only frontier on a volatility-target grid. Before resampling, the raw panel is recentered so that its full-sample means equal the MATF baseline at the prior mean; the comparison therefore isolates sampling bandwidth from level, and the raw estimator is not penalized for a different point estimate. This estimator is mathematically identical to a Grinold-Kroner construction whose per-asset point estimate is the historical sample mean. \textbf{2) MATF factor-structured estimator:} each draw resamples paired factor-return and residual panels, re-estimates $\Sigma_F$ and $D$, draws factor Sharpe ratios from the structural prior, and reconstructs the asset moments as $\boldsymbol\mu = r_f + \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\boldsymbol\lambda$, $\Sigma = \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\Sigma_F\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^\intercal + D$ with the loading matrix held fixed, treating the better-identified loadings as fixed at leading order \citep{Shanken1992,ChopraZiemba1993}.

We use the stationary block bootstrap of \cite{PolitisRomano1994} with mean block length $12$ months and minimum block length $3$ months, block starts aligned so the quarterly observation phase survives resampling, $B=500$ draws. The Sharpe-ratio prior is multivariate normal, $\mathrm{SR}^{(b)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol m_{\mathrm{SR}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{SR}}^2\,\rho_F)$ with $\rho_F$ the factor correlation matrix and
\[
\boldsymbol m_{\mathrm{SR}} = (0.40,\, 0.25,\, 0.40,\, 0.40,\, 0.25,\, 0.25,\, 0.15,\, 0.15,\, 0.60,\, 0.25,\, 0.00)
\]
in the factor order of Exhibit~\ref{tab:nine_factors}---the production equilibrium Sharpe-ratio vector of the 2026-Q2 configuration, in which each factor inherits its family's prior, so Credit EM carries the Credit prior and both carry factors the carry prior, and the prior mean is the framework's own calibration rather than a separate object. We set $\sigma_{\mathrm{SR}} = 0.10$ because at the effective sample size $T_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 25$ the sample-mean Sharpe-ratio estimator has asymptotic standard deviation $1/\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{eff}}} \approx 0.20$ \citep{Lo2002}; the prior is twice as tight as the historical record, informationally equivalent to four times the sample, and its $95\%$ interval of $\pm 0.20$ spans the dispersion of published cross-asset Sharpe-ratio estimates \citep{Ilmanen2011}.

Exhibit~\ref{tab:sr_sensitivity} reports the robustness of the bandwidth reduction to $\sigma_{\mathrm{SR}}$: the finding holds across $\sigma_{\mathrm{SR}} \in [0.05, 0.15]$, and the limit $\sigma_{\mathrm{SR}} = 0.20$ approaches the sample-mean bandwidth because a prior as wide as the data adds no information.

\paragraph*{Standard Errors of the Three Constructions.} Each methodology writes the expected return as a linear function of an estimated input vector, so its standard error follows from the variance of a linear form. The sample mean over $T_{\mathrm{eff}} = 25$ effective annual observations gives $\sigma_i/\sqrt{T_{\mathrm{eff}}} = 3.25\%$ for US equity at the Q2 volatility of $16.2\%$. The Grinold-Kroner construction sums four component forecasts per asset; with component standard errors of $0.1\%$ (payout yield), $1.5\%$ (real growth), $1.0\%$ (inflation), and $2.0\%$ (valuation reversion) and a uniform pairwise correlation of $\rho = 0.5$ reflecting shared macroeconomic drivers, the variance of the sum gives $3.77\%$ \citep{ChopraZiemba1993}, and the GK variance does not shrink with $T$ because each component is a forecast of a separate process. The MATF estimator writes $\hat R_i = r_f + \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_i^{\intercal}(\mathrm{SR} \odot \boldsymbol\sigma_F)$; for an asset concentrated on a single factor the standard error reduces to $|\hat\beta_{i,f^\star}| \cdot \sigma_{\mathrm{SR}} \cdot \sigma_{F,f^\star}$, which for US equity ($\hat\beta_{\mathrm{Eq}} = 0.99$, $\sigma_{F,\mathrm{Eq}} = 15.5\%$) is $1.52\%$. The three standard errors stand in the ratio $3.2 : 3.8 : 1.5$, and the bootstrap of the main text confirms the implied ratio of roughly $2{:}1$ between the non-factor methods and MATF.

\paragraph*{The Residual-Norm Distribution.} The same bootstrap yields the sampling distribution of the consistency residual under per-asset construction: applying the annihilator of Appendix~A to each raw-estimator draw gives per-asset residual medians from $5$ basis points (global government) to $180$ basis points (gold), a median portfolio violation norm of $4.2\%$, and a 95th percentile of $7.4\%$. These are the figures quoted in the main text; the per-asset distribution is tabulated in the replication package.

\subsection*{Appendix C: Estimation of the Factor Risk Premia}\label{app:premia}

For the equity, rates, and credit factors we use fundamental market-implied valuation models; for the structural factors we use equilibrium Sharpe ratios. Each fundamental premium is blended $50/50$ between its current market-implied value and its long-run equilibrium anchor, applied uniformly to the regional columns and the global aggregate; the blend weight is the declared control of Section ``The MATF-CMA Construction''. All illustrations in this appendix are as of 30 June 2026 on the 2026-Q2 cut.

\paragraph*{Equity.} We derive the equity premium from the payout- and cyclically-adjusted earnings yield (P-CAEY) of \cite{HaghaniWhite2024}, which improves on the CAPE of \cite{CampbellShiller1988} by compounding retained earnings forward at the prevailing cyclically-adjusted yield: with $d_s$ the payout ratio and $K=10$ years,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p_adj}
	E_s^{P\text{-}adj} = d_s \tilde{E}_{s} + (1 - d_s) \tilde{E}_{s} (1 + \text{CAEY}_s)^{(t - s)}, \qquad
	\text{P-CAEY}_t = \frac{\tfrac{1}{K}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} E_{t-k}^{P\text{-}adj}}{P_t},
\end{equation}
and the regional premium is the excess over the real risk-free rate, $\lambda_{Equity,r} = \text{P-CAEY}_r - r_{f,r}^{real}$, with the real rate taken from a cascade preferring market-implied inflation (TIPS yields, inflation swaps, breakevens, trailing CPI in that order). The global premium aggregates regional premia by market-capitalization weights. Regional equity sleeves are not pure equity exposures, so the excess CMA of region $r$ prices the estimated global-equity loading at the global premium and the complement at the region's own premium,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regional_cma}
	\text{CMA}_r^{excess} = \hat{\beta}_r^{eq} \lambda_{Equity}^{Global} + (1 - \hat{\beta}_r^{eq}) \lambda_{Equity,r} + \sum_{j \neq eq} \hat{\beta}_{r,j} \lambda_j ,
\end{equation}
which is the regional valuation blend channel $b_i$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:cma2a}. The emerging-market regional earnings yield carries the declared $-200$ basis-point discretionary adjustment of the main text.

\paragraph*{Rates.} The rates premium combines the OIS-implied term premium with the roll-down carry of the curve: per country, $TP_{10,c} = y_{10,c} - \text{OIS}_{5Y5Y,c}$ and $RD_{10,c} = (y_{10,c} - y_{7,c}) D_{10}/T_{roll}$ with $D_{10} \approx 7$ and $T_{roll} = 3$, so $\lambda_{Rates,c} = TP_{10,c} + RD_{10,c}$, aggregated over the six-country futures basket by liquidity weights \citep{Ilmanen2011}. This formulation is model-free and available daily for all G10 currencies, properties that VAR-based latent-factor estimates such as \cite{ACM2013} do not satisfy in a multi-currency setting. Country government-bond sleeves carry different durations from the volatility-targeted factor, so the rates bracket of the sleeve CMA is rescaled by $\sigma_c/\sigma^*_{Rates}$, with the non-rates contributions unscaled because their betas are estimated on the asset's own returns and already reflect its volatility level.

\paragraph*{Credit.} The credit factor is constructed from CDX IG and HY index trackers with weights $66/34$. The CDS spread decomposes as expected loss plus credit risk premium, $CRP \approx s - PD^{\mathbb{P}} \times LGD$, with through-the-cycle default parameters from Moody's corporate default studies (Exhibit~\ref{tab:actuarial}); the current-market CRP is blended $50/50$ with its long-run average per the framework convention.

The published premium applies the prior Sharpe ratio to the factor's realized volatility, a 60-month EWMA initialised at the target, so the current values of Exhibit~\ref{tab:nine_factors} move with the risk regime while the static anchors of Exhibit~\ref{tab:sharpe_cal} do not. The rates-volatility factor prices the short-volatility exposure embedded in corporate assets: more than $80\%$ of equity variance is attributable to cash flows beyond ten years, where discount-rate risk dominates \citep{Goncalves2025}, and every major asset class exhibits negative beta to swaption straddle returns \citep{Morris2026}. \paragraph*{Benchmarks.} The eight policy benchmarks combine four risk profiles (Income, Low, Balanced, Growth) with two universe variants. The without-alternatives variant splits bonds and equities at $100/0$, $70/30$, $40/60$, and $0/100$; the with-alternatives variant adds $10$--$40\%$ alternatives funded proportionally from bonds and equities, which preserves the bond-equity ratio at every alternatives level. Instrument weights are the product of the class allocation and within-class decomposition weights taken from the Bloomberg Multiverse composition (fixed income), the MSCI ACWI regional breakdown (equities), and an indicative allocation (alternatives); the full weight table is part of the replication package \citep{MatfCmaCode2026}.

\paragraph*{Optimization.} The SAA solve maximizes the expected active excess return subject to a tracking-error constraint,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saa1}
	\max_{\boldsymbol w} \; \boldsymbol\mu_e^{\intercal}\,(\boldsymbol w - \boldsymbol w^{(bm)})
	\quad \text{s.t.} \quad
	(\boldsymbol w - \boldsymbol w^{(bm)})^{\intercal} \Sigma\, (\boldsymbol w - \boldsymbol w^{(bm)}) \le \sigma^2_{target}, \quad \boldsymbol 1^\intercal \boldsymbol w = 1,
\end{equation}
with $\sigma_{target} = 1.5\%$, individual bounds of $\pm 50\%$ around the benchmark weight, and the covariance of Equation~\eqref{eq:cma_ii}; additional group and turnover constraints follow \cite{Rosaa2026}. The objective is stated in excess returns deliberately: under the budget constraint the reference cash rate cancels identically from the active return, so the optimum is invariant to the cash anchor and one optimization serves every reference currency, with totals restored by adding $r_f^{ref}$ for reporting. Within the tracking-error budget the optimizer lifts every mandate's expected return above its benchmark by tilting toward the highest factor-implied CMAs, and in the resulting factor decomposition the Equity factor remains the dominant risk driver of every profile---multi-asset diversification at the asset-class level does not eliminate factor concentration. The eight-mandate frontier exhibit and the per-mandate factor exposures are reproduced in the replication package.

\paragraph*{Multi-Currency Construction.} Equities and alternatives are treated as currency unhedged and fixed income as hedged to the reference currency. The unhedged regression target subtracts the local cash rate and adds the spot FX return, so that under long-horizon uncovered interest parity its expectation coincides with the hedged target; loadings are then re-estimated against reference-currency returns, and the difference across reference currencies traces entirely to the FX-factor covariance term of unhedged assets, while hedged assets carry currency-invariant loadings. Factor premia, the factor covariance, and the asset clusters are estimated once and held fixed across currencies; the reported total CMAs then differ across reference currencies almost entirely through the reference cash rate. Combined with the anchor invariance of the optimization above, hedged mandates in different reference currencies hold the same book by construction.

\paragraph*{Loading Estimator.} The loadings are estimated by the sign-constrained clustered group-lasso regression with prior-centered regularization, with the sign constraints of Exhibit~\ref{tab:sign_constraints} and asset clusters computed once on the USD correlation matrix and held fixed across currency fits. The estimator, its statistical properties, and the open-source implementation are developed in the companion methodology papers \citep{SeppFactorLassoCSDA2026,SeppFactorLassoJSS2026}; the replication repository pins the estimator version and full configuration of the frozen production cut.

\subsection*{Appendix E: Admission Sensitivity Detail}\label{app:admission_detail}

The one-at-a-time sensitivity behind Section ``From Dials to Weights'' ranks the six admissions by varying each sleeve's weight over $w_i \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$ with all others at the production policy: Private Equity and Gold are the two most consequential single assumptions, moving the claimed Sharpe ratio of the Balanced mandate by $0.070$ and $0.039$ around the production value of $0.39$, while every other admission moves the claim by less than $0.01$ and leaves the allocation pinned by the box and tracking-error constraints; the full tornado exhibit is reproduced in the replication package.

The squared-Sharpe accounting behind Section ``Decision Two''---the frictionless ceiling of $0.53$, the attainable content of $0.21$, the raw claim of $1.44$, the projected claim of $0.48$, and the per-carrier premium-like shares including the $79\%$ insurance-linked share---is derived in \citet{SeppKastenholzFAJ2026}, and its graphical version is reproduced in the replication package. Exhibit~\ref{fig:cap3_implied_premia} is the identification companion of Cap~3. The implied premium revisions of the published CMA vector against the calibrated premia look absurd in basis points---hundreds of basis points on Commodities, Rates Volatility, and FX---until standardized by the identification scale $\sqrt{[(\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{\intercal}\boldsymbol D^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta})^{-1}]_{jj}}$: every standardized deviation stays inside one identification unit, so the production admissions are statistically indistinguishable from premium revisions on the poorly spanned factors. An admission in a badly spanned direction is an unauditable premium claim, which is the identification argument for bounding the aggregate.
