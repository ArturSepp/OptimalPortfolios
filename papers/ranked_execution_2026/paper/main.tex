\title{Execution-Constrained Portfolio Rebalancing: Policy Domains, Search and Numerical Reliability}
\author{Artur Sepp}
\address{LGT}
\begin{abstract}
Executing a target portfolio requires choosing actual trades under funding,
exposure, minimum-size and ticket constraints. We specify these choices as
labelled executable intervals, separating compulsory actions, common
feasibility preparation and tracking-risk minimisation. A correlated two-asset
example shows that restricting movements to target corridors can raise annual
tracking error from 43.59 to 100 basis points. We compare joint mixed-integer
optimisation (Exact Execution), bounded support search (Ranked Execution) and
a documented Kernel Search adaptation on the same prepared domain. A retained
313-case cohort favours Exact's median computation, but a frozen prospective
study reveals different limits. On 18 larger-candidate synthetic cases, Exact
and Kernel return audited portfolios in every call; Ranked returns two within
its declared process ceiling. Kernel improves Exact's 60-second incumbent in
11 cases, ties four and loses three. All three 600-second Exact controls remain
unresolved. Two larger externally sourced risk cases instead favour Exact.
Equivalent numerical controls expose original-unit bound inconsistencies despite
native optimal labels; balanced representations avoid those contradictions in
the tested cases. The evidence distinguishes economic restrictions, search
quality and numerical acceptance. It supports conditional execution comparisons,
not universal method superiority or realised investment-performance claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Selecting movements from an existing portfolio differs from selecting the
assets in a new portfolio. A limited order list may need to fund purchases,
exit ineligible holdings and respect overlapping exposures. The value of a
trade depends on the other trades that can accompany it. Minimum delivered
orders and minimum final holdings further restrict those combinations.

This paper asks how the declared execution domain, support search and numerical
representation affect executable delivery. The objective is tracking variance
against a supplied raw target. Alpha, turnover penalties, transaction costs and
upstream target estimation are outside that execution objective. We compare
methods only after resolving the same mandatory actions and feasible base.

Our contribution has three parts. First, an explicit state formulation separates
trade-count semantics, preparation and economic restrictions; an independent
counterexample establishes that target corridors need not preserve the full-domain
optimum. Second, controlled representations distinguish the mathematical objective
from native stopping diagnostics and an independently audited delivery. Third,
frozen supplied-input experiments identify operating limits of three declared
implementations, including failures and outcomes against a preferred method.
The disjunctive and perspective constructions are established tools, and the
primary/fallback wrapper is not claimed as a new optimisation principle.

The evidence is deliberately not a single ranking. Exact is computationally
attractive in the retained small-candidate cohort. Kernel frequently finds
better incumbents in the new synthetic stress cells, but loses in other cells
and in the larger external-risk cases. Ranked often returns no result within
its process ceiling. These observations motivate an explicit separation of
policy-domain costs, search limits and numerical reliability.

\section{Related work}
Index tracking and sparse portfolio selection provide the closest optimisation
context. \citet{beasley2003} study an evolutionary tracking heuristic;
\citet{canakgoz2009} develop mixed-integer tracking and enhanced-indexation
models. \citet{mutunge2018} examine cardinality-constrained tracking portfolios,
and \citet{strub2018} study their construction and rebalancing. Our cardinality
counts movements from an existing book rather than terminal holdings. Hard
execution rules also differ from a trading-cost objective, although fixed costs
and minimum orders belong to the established nonconvex portfolio-modelling
family described by \citet{lobo2007}.

Finite unions of executable intervals admit standard disjunctive extended
formulations \citep{vielma2015}. Perspective methods strengthen quadratic
relaxations with indicator variables \citep{frangioni2006,gunluk2010}.
We use those properties without claiming that the coupled portfolio relaxation
is ideal. The ridge-regularised sparse model of \citet{bertsimas2022} provides
another scalable approach, but adding its regulariser would change the objective
here and therefore would not constitute a matched comparator.

Kernel Search explores restricted mixed-integer subproblems
\citep{kernel2012,kernel2014}. Our comparator adapts that framework to the same
quadratic executable-state model, and explicitly describes departures from the
published algorithms. Ranked Execution instead constructs and replaces supports,
then jointly sizes them. Neither restricted-support search nor support exchange
is new. The empirical question is how these particular implementations behave
under identical economic inputs and independent starts.

\section{Execution domain and policy restrictions}\label{sec:domain}
\subsection{Inputs and constraints}
Let $c,m,w\in\R^N$ be original current, raw target and delivered weights,
measured as fractions of net asset value (NAV). Settlement cash has index $k$.
With supplied annual covariance $\Sigma\succeq0$, minimise
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
 f(w)=(w-m)^\top\Sigma(w-m),\qquad \TE(w)=\sqrt{f(w)}.
\end{equation}
Annual tracking-error basis points are $10^4\TE(w)$. The long-only hard domain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard}
 \mathbf1^\top w=1,\qquad L\le w\le U,\qquad a\le Aw\le b.
\end{equation}
The exposure matrix $A$ can contain overlapping and fractional memberships.
Cash has no ticket, but retains bounds and covariance contributions.

An ordinary noncash movement is zero or at least $\delta_i$ in absolute size;
a final holding is zero or at least $\theta_i$. These are different rules:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minima}
 w_i=c_i\ \text{or}\ |w_i-c_i|\ge\delta_i,\qquad
 w_i=0\ \text{or}\ w_i\ge\theta_i.
\end{equation}
Tickets count actual delivered movements. An explicit compulsory minimum-size
exemption does not remove its ticket or waive its holding floor.

Before solving, reconcile every declared group with the compiled rows. A missing
membership column is an input error, not a zero vector. An explicitly acknowledged
empty group is redundant only when its bounds contain zero; otherwise it is
contradictory. Reconciliation records source identifiers, coefficients and bounds.
It addresses a problem a return audit alone cannot detect: an omitted constraint
would also be absent from that audit. No coefficient or tolerance is changed.

\subsection{Mandatory actions and common preparation}
The consumer resolves lifecycle changes, instructions and ordinary hard-bound
repairs into a proposed base. Every genuine noncash base--current movement has
an explicit mandatory category. A compulsory action can pin a weight or retain
an admissible sizing interval; its original movement is counted in either case.
Numerical residuals within the unchanged zero threshold do not create a new
compulsory action.

Individual instructions may violate joint funding or exposures. Common
preparation first retains an already audited base unchanged. Otherwise it searches
the corridor domain and then eligible full bounds. In the full-domain stage it
minimises corridor excursion before gross noncash intervention and the number
of added repair instruments. A strictly convex squared-intervention objective
breaks continuous ties within the selected discrete pattern. Target tracking
risk does not choose repairs. Sequential numerical guards qualify this priority
order; it is not exact symbolic lexicographic arithmetic.

Additional repairs are pinned, categorised and counted before risk search.
All comparison arms receive the same resulting $q$, bounds, exemptions and
mandatory declarations. An optional allowance $B$ gives $K=M+B$ under that
explicit convention. An independently specified fixed total cap must instead
remain fixed; preparation cannot silently enlarge it. Preparation failures stay
in the denominator and are not automatically proofs of infeasibility.

\subsection{Corridor versus full hard domain}
For an ordinary optional name, a target corridor is
\[
 [\min(q_i,m_i),\max(q_i,m_i)]\cap[L_i,U_i].
\]
Resolved instruction and compulsory intervals take precedence. The \emph{Full}
domain uses eligible hard bounds without the ordinary corridor restriction.
Our algorithm comparisons use the same \emph{Corridor} domain. Full comparisons
are explicitly different policy problems.

A corridor can exclude the best execution combination. Let cash be the third
coordinate, take one noncash ticket, bounds $[0,1]$, minimum trade $0.005$ and
holding floor $0.0025$, and set
\[
 c=(.2,.2,.6),\quad m=(.3,.3,.4),\quad
 \Sigma=.01\begin{pmatrix}1&.9&0\\.9&1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If only the first risky asset trades, $x=w_1-.3$ gives
$f(w)=.01(x^2-.18x+.01)$. The corridor minimum is $x=0$, yielding
$w=(.3,.2,.5)$ and TE 100 bp. Full permits $x=.09$, yielding
$w=(.39,.2,.41)$ and TE 43.588989 bp. Trading the second asset is symmetric;
no trade is worse. These scalar cases exhaust the ticket choices independently.
The overshoot offsets an untraded correlated underweight. Thus the corridor is
an economic policy restriction, not a generally lossless reduction.

\section{Formulation and solution methods}\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Labelled executable states}
For each noncash name $i$, compile intervals $[\ell_{ij},u_{ij}]$, $j\in J_i$,
by intersecting permitted movements with the hard bounds and the trade/holding
rules. The no-trade state is a point with label $t_{ij}=0$; active states have
$t_{ij}=1$. A full exit can be a separate state. Mandatory declarations remove
inadmissible no-trade states. Let $I_O$ denote optional names.

Introduce binary selectors $z_{ij}$ and contributions $v_{ij}$. Exact Execution
is the joint mixed-integer quadratic model for \eqref{eq:objective} and
\eqref{eq:hard}, with
\begin{align}
 \sum_{j\in J_i}z_{ij}&=1,&
 \ell_{ij}z_{ij}\le v_{ij}&\le u_{ij}z_{ij},&
 w_i&=\sum_{j\in J_i}v_{ij},\label{eq:states}\\
 \sum_{i\ne k}\sum_{j\in J_i}t_{ij}z_{ij}&\le K,&
 \sum_{i\in I_O}\sum_{j\in J_i}t_{ij}z_{ij}&\le B.\label{eq:counts}
\end{align}
An unspecified capacity row is omitted. Exactly one selected interval supplies
each weight; all inactive contributions are zero. Conversely every admissible
labelled portfolio supplies such selectors and contributions. This proves
projection equivalence with the declared domain, using a standard extended
formulation \citep{vielma2015}. It does not prove ideality of the coupled
continuous relaxation. The supplement gives the complete argument and
separates this nominal domain from finite-precision acceptance.

\subsection{Exact, Ranked and Kernel Search}
Exact Execution solves the joint state and sizing model. ``Exact'' identifies
that formulation, not a promise that every time-limited call proves optimality.
Its evaluated default is non-perspective. The frozen native controls retain
one thread, relative-gap target $10^{-4}$ and an absolute target equivalent to
$10^{-12}$ original variance. Audited callback incumbents are retained, and a
bounded fixed-state refinement can repair a numerical boundary candidate.
Refinement stays inside the same constraints and audit envelope.

Ranked Execution is a bounded support search. It computes cash-funded scalar
and pair priorities, forms candidate supports and jointly sizes their selected
states under all hard rows. Search includes mandatory-only sizing, greedy
construction, repair supports, single replacements, conditional coordinated
moves, perspective-guided restarts and screened three-position combinations.
A cache avoids repeating support sizing. Identifier and state order resolve
ties. The released rescue budget is 24, mandatory-assignment budget 32 and
improvement-pass budget three. The supplement gives the ordered pseudocode,
including branch budgets and stopping; no new stopping rule is introduced here.

Perspective guidance splits the funded covariance as
$Q=Q_0+\operatorname{diag}(d)$ with $Q_0\succeq0$ and $d\ge0$.
For optional occupancy $y_i$, replace $d_ix_i^2$ by $d_ix_i^2/y_i$ with its
closed convex extension at zero. It equals the original quadratic at integral
occupancy and strengthens the continuous relaxation
\citep{frangioni2006,gunluk2010}. An approximate conic primal value alone is not
a lower bound. Ranked's separately corrected relaxation-bound routine and
native mixed-integer bound diagnostics are distinct mechanisms.

Kernel Search uses the same state formulation, independent prepared start,
objective and audit. Following the framework of \citet{kernel2012,kernel2014},
our adaptation ranks names by fractional trade occupancy in the perspective
relaxation, selects an initial kernel of at most $\max(24,2B)$ names and partitions
the remainder into equally sized buckets. Restricted joint solves retain all
mandatory actions. A bucket solve requires a bucket trade and uses an incumbent
objective cutoff; selected bucket names join the kernel. The remaining allowance
is divided among remaining stages. One pass is made, without ageing or removal.
Quadratic guidance, occupancy ordering and instrument-level state groups are
explicit adaptations, not a literal replication of the original tracking model.
Restricted subproblem bounds are never presented as global bounds.

The methods therefore use comparable economic problems but different search
architectures. The study does not compare the strongest attainable versions of
these algorithm families. For $T$ distinct support trials and at most $H$
mandatory assignments, Ranked attempts at most $TH$ fixed-state sizing problems,
in addition to guidance. Shortlist generation and dense covariance operations
also cost time. There is no proved $O(NB)$ or approximation-ratio guarantee.
Factor-plus-diagonal inputs do not make this implementation linear-memory:
funded model construction and QIS reporting materialise dense matrices.

\subsection{Audited execution policy}
A separate operational policy attempts Exact first and calls Ranked only when
the primary cannot provide an acceptable native candidate. It checks original
inputs, callback/native failures and available bound consistency. The prepared
base alone is not evidence of successful native search. Fallback must pass the
same audit, or the policy fails. This rule does not run both methods and select
the better objective. Its preparation still depends on the common solver
infrastructure, so it is not independent protection against a backend outage.
The older operational MIQP has different policy/objective semantics and is not
a matched arm in this paper.

\section{Numerical representation and acceptance}\label{sec:numerics}
Eliminate fixed coordinates and cash using a funded reference $r$ and columns
$e_i-e_k$ in $D$. Then $w=r+Dx$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:funded}
 f(r+Dx)=x^\top Qx+2g^\top x+f(r),\quad
 Q=D^\top\Sigma D,\quad g=D^\top\Sigma(r-m).
\end{equation}
All instrument and group rows, including cash bounds, undergo the same
substitution. Funding elimination preserves the full covariance objective.

\subsection{Objective origin and native bounds}
A positive affine transformation $\alpha f+\beta$ preserves the minimiser,
but changes the relative-gap denominator. For incumbent value $F_U$ and lower
bound $F_L$ and denominator floor $\eta>0$, the transformed gap has the form
\[
 \frac{\alpha|F_U-F_L|}{\max(\eta,|\alpha F_U+\beta|)}.
\]
For $F_U=10^{-10}$, $F_L=9\times10^{-11}$, removing a constant $10^{-3}$ changes
the relative gap from $0.1$ to approximately $10^{-8}$. The absolute gap
$10^{-11}$ still exceeds $10^{-12}$. This analytical pair shows why restoring
only the minimiser-equivalent expression is insufficient for relative stopping.
It is not fabricated native solver output. The implementation preserves the
constant in \eqref{eq:funded} and restores variance units in diagnostics.

Absolute or relative native stopping may terminate a call \citep{mosek}.
A native optimal label, a feasible delivery and an independently established
optimum remain different claims. Moreover, a better native vector can fail
portfolio acceptance. Its native gap cannot be attributed to a different,
worse audited portfolio. We retain both objectives and gaps, and check native
bounds against all available audited witnesses in the same domain.

\subsection{Equivalent cones and fixed rows}
In the scaled objective, basis-point coordinates $x_{\rm bp}$ give the identity
\[
 (x_{\rm bp}/100)^\top Q(x_{\rm bp}/100)
 =x_{\rm bp}^\top Qx_{\rm bp}/10^4.
\]
Four isolated
24/72-optional-name controls retain identical state rows, the linear term,
constant and native settings. Both representations return audited portfolios
and native integer-optimal labels. Nevertheless unbalanced-graph bounds exceed
better audited balanced-graph witnesses by $7.630\times10^{-11}$ and
$5.439\times10^{-10}$ original variance, above the unchanged comparison scale.
Balanced graphs show no contradiction at that scale. The TE differences are
economically small; the diagnostic inconsistency is the relevant mechanism.

Fixed-coordinate elimination can make a group row constant. The compiler removes
it only if its coefficients are exactly zero and its constant satisfies both
compiled endpoints. Every nonconstant row and constant contradiction remains.
Eight graph controls compare keeping/removing such rows, for upper/lower limits
just inside and outside the existing audit envelope. In this runtime both graph
variants solve the inside-envelope cases and match an independent active-set
reference; all outside-envelope variants are infeasible. The historical sizing
failure does not reproduce, so no delivery benefit for row elimination is claimed
from these controls. Detailed observations are in the supplement.

\section{Frozen studies and accounting}\label{sec:design}
All studies use the released OptimalPortfolios 9.2.0 wheel, verified by hash,
with QIS 5.44.4, CVXPY 1.9.2, MOSEK 11.2.3 and Clarabel 0.11.1
\citep{op,qis,cvxpy,clarabel,mosek}. Inputs, numerical source hashes, ordering,
seeds and budgets are frozen before each evaluation. No solver is tuned after
observing that cohort. AI tools assisted code and manuscript preparation;
retained tests, independent references and raw observations support verification.

Original-unit audits retain $10^{-7}$ NAV feasibility and $10^{-8}$ trade-zero
tolerances. The descriptive TE equality band is $10^{-4}$ annual bp, separate
from feasibility. Comparisons retain failures, open gaps and both signs of TE
differences. Native statuses are independently checked on six tiny controls
by enumerating 206 supports, with none unresolved; this does not certify larger
cases. Primary-fault injections are reported separately from natural fallback.

\subsection{Retained 313-case cohort}
The ETF case study contains 219 monthly decisions, July 2008--September 2026,
from a retrospectively selected 302-ETF universe approved in October 2026.
Current books are the previous supplied model weights, normalised and drifted
through the intervening month. Each method receives the same current book;
methods do not evolve their own holdings recursively.

Targets are an adaptation of the multi-asset allocation framework of
\citet{sepp2026}, not a literal replication of its index universe. Their frozen
upstream pipeline uses monthly eleven-factor fits, momentum/low-beta signals,
corrected log-excess reconstruction, noncash group tracking-variance utility
coefficient 10, turnover coefficient 0.4 and 10\% instrument caps. Targets were
produced with OP 7.21.0, QIS 5.33.1 and FactorLasso 1.0.2 \citep{factorlasso};
execution uses OP 9.2.0.
These upstream penalties do not enter \eqref{eq:objective}. The numerical archive
supplies targets and risk estimates; public execution replication does not refit
the upstream model or reproduce licensed market-data processing.

Execution bands are 0--10\% liquidity, 25--45\% fixed income, 35--65\% equity
and 5--15\% alternatives, with fractional exposures. Ordinary orders have a
25 NAV bp minimum; equity/alternative holdings have a 50 NAV bp floor. The
optional allowance is ten. Only zero to five effective optional candidates
remain per decision: this is primarily a preparation/mandatory-execution case
study, not evidence of difficult optional search.

The retained synthetic sample has 24 broad cases and two tiny controls. A
separately frozen fresh cohort has 64 broad cases and four tiny controls, using
two new seeds, diffuse/exit-rich targets, overlapping/disjoint groups and
wide/tight funding. Six optional tickets and 144 nominal optional assets
already occur in the earlier grid. All retain two compulsory exits, minimum
ordinary trade 0.5\% and holding floor 0.25\%. Historical cases informed prior
development; fresh cases did not trigger subsequent tuning. Factorial variants
share draws and are not independent market observations.

Independent Exact, Ranked and the actual primary/fallback wrapper are measured.
Exact receives ten seconds. Explicit Ranked-started and 60/600-second controls
remain separate, with incumbent-generation costs charged. End-to-end timings
include preparation but exclude process imports and file reads in this cohort.

\subsection{Prospective difficulty and external-risk sample}
The new protocol has 18 synthetic cells: 300/1,000/3,000 eligible noncash names,
10/30/100 optional tickets and two predeclared profiles. Current portfolios
hold 96 names and 6\% cash; targets add 100 names and hold 2\% cash. The 0.5\%
minimum trade and 0.25\% holding floor remain feasible. Wide/disjoint/diffuse
cases declare two compulsory exits; tight/overlapping/exit-rich cases declare
six. Seed, profile and economic features are bundled, with one seed per profile.
Nominal size also changes risk draws. These cells are illustrative, not a
factorial identification of individual causes or population superiority.

The effective Corridor candidate counts are 155 and 190, with 200--280 ticket
states. Nominal universe size, held positions and effective search dimension
are therefore reported separately. The same factor-plus-diagonal covariance
is supplied in dense form for three representation controls.

Four external-risk cases use OR-Library's 31- and 457-stock panels, at
observation cuts 145 and 290 \citep{beasley2003,canakgoz2009,orlibrary}.
Point-in-time QIS log-return EWMA covariance uses span 52 and annualisation 52;
artificial weekly dates are assigned because calendar labels are absent.
Holdings, targets, cash variance and all execution constraints are synthetic.
These are risk-input adaptations, not replications of the original tracking
objectives. Owner prices and derived covariance arrays are excluded from
redistribution pending reuse terms; source checksums and public regeneration
code are supplied. The analytic corridor case completes the 23-case sample.

Main arms are Exact60, Kernel60 and released Ranked with a 180-second external
process ceiling. The three tight, 30-ticket cells add Exact10, Exact600,
Dense10 and Full60; the analytic case adds Full60. There are 82 declared
attempts. All calls run serially, in rotated order, with one solver/numerical
library thread and independent starts. Common preparation has a 30-second
allowance. Native allowances are not hard wall-clock deadlines: separate
process ceilings and all failures are recorded.

This cohort records engine, preparation, representation and whole-process times,
including imports and reporting; these differ from the retained cohort's timing
scope. A killed worker without a returned portfolio is a failed delivery, even
if it held an internal incumbent. Ranked receives no new anytime interface.
The prepared base is already audited in every new case, so time to first native
feasibility can merely measure acceptance of that start. This is not a matched
anytime-interface experiment. Whole-worker memory includes dense risk reporting;
missing killed-worker measurements are not imputed.

Ranked fallbacks. There are no natural fallback observations in this cohort.

\section{Implications and limitations}
The execution domain, solver and audit answer separate questions. Corridors
restrict economic choices; an unfinished solver limits the search within those
choices; numerical acceptance determines whether a returned vector is usable.
The counterexample and bound controls show why these distinctions have practical
consequences even when two expressions share the same mathematical minimiser.

The evidence supports neither general Ranked superiority nor a universal Exact
speed/quality advantage. Kernel's synthetic gains are substantial in several
cells, but reverse in others. The tested Exact default is not perspective
strengthened, and Ranked's released interface often misses its declared deadline.
A new formulation or stopping interface would require separate development and
fresh evaluation, not adjustment of this frozen sample.

The ETF universe is retrospective, estimates are supplied and monthly decisions
are dependent. Internally generated holdouts are not independent external
validation; the external-risk adaptation also has synthetic execution rules.
All new bases already pass preparation, so preparation difficulty is not
identified. Timings describe one host and declared controls, not universal
speed guarantees. Hardware and wall-clock cutoffs can change returned incumbents.

No new primary/fallback arm was run on the difficulty sample. Its standalone
failures cannot be converted into an observed fallback frequency, and the
wrapper does not guarantee the better of two methods. Nor do these conditional
experiments support own-holdings rolling, realised return, Sharpe or transaction-
cost claims. The usable result is a reproducible account of policy costs,
computational limits and numerical checks, including inconvenient outcomes.

bundle provides source, protocols, checksums, original-input audits and reference
summaries. Separate reviewed archives contain synthetic inputs, raw observations
and the supplied de-identified ETF decisions; opaque asset labels provide
no guarantee against recognition of numerical histories. Private fund data,
identity mappings and vendor histories are excluded. OR-Library files are
retrieved from their owner. Persistent deposit identifiers remain pending.
The public code is in OptimalPortfolios \citep{op}, with QIS analytics
\citep{qis}; the exact evaluated wheel and source hashes remain the scientific
reference regardless of subsequent API naming changes.
