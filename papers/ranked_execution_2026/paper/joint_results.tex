\paragraph{Coverage and scale}\label{sec:joint}\label{sec:breadth}
All 313 cases prepare successfully, and all 1,356 arm deliveries pass the
independent original-unit audit. The primary/fallback policy uses matched
MIQP on all 313 cases and reproduces the independently run MIQP weights
exactly; no natural fallback occurs. The ETF cases have 0--5 effective
optional candidates against an allowance of ten. Their ticket cap is
therefore nonbinding for optional search. The existing broad synthetic
cases have 20--41 candidates; the fresh broad holdout has 22--104.

\paragraph{Quality}
On the ETF sample, V2 ties independent MIQP on 207 cases and has lower TE
on 12 beyond the descriptive band. The largest V2 advantage is only
0.000941 bp; these differences do not establish an economic advantage.
All 26 existing synthetic cases tie, and increasing the independent
MIQP allowance from ten to 60 or 600 seconds changes no delivered TE.

On the fresh holdout, independent MIQP ties V2 on 64 of 68 cases and has
lower TE on four, by up to 2.457878 bp. All four are broad cases with six
optional tickets, overlapping groups and tight funding/exposure bands
(Table~\ref{tab:misses}). Three have exit-rich targets. Both outputs are
feasible; these are differences in search quality, not waived constraints.
The holdout outcomes did not trigger a search correction or parameter change.
Separate V2-started MIQP calls also recover the lower-TE solutions, but
charge the cost of generating V2's incumbent.

\paragraph{Computation}
Independent MIQP has lower median E2E time in each cohort. In the fresh
holdout its median is 0.616 seconds versus 4.591 for V2; the 95th percentiles
are 1.935 and 25.609 seconds. The corresponding maxima are 2.929 and
35.024 seconds. The actual policy's holdout median is 0.651 seconds.
The measured primary calls do not exhaust their ten-second allowances.
These are serial measurements on one host, not universal speed guarantees.

\paragraph{Numerical controls and stopping diagnostics}
No native bound contradicts a better audited cross-arm witness at the
unchanged $10^{-12}$ variance comparison scale. All six independent tiny
enumerations finish: 206 supports, none unresolved. Their maximum absolute
delivery difference is $3.55\times10^{-9}$ annual bp. These controls do not
independently certify the larger instances.

Relative-gap settings must be interpreted together with absolute stopping.
Two near-zero-risk ETF cases have reported native relative gaps above
$10^{-4}$; the maximum is 0.009671. In that case the native objective is
$5.784\times10^{-12}$ variance and the absolute native gap is only
$5.594\times10^{-14}$. The unchanged native absolute-gap allowance is
$10^{-8}$ in the scaled objective, equivalent to $10^{-12}$ variance.
Thus a relative-gap target is not an unconditional delivery guarantee,
even with the correct objective origin. The raw values remain in the
replication records, and neither the policy nor the paper upgrades them
into an independent optimality claim.

\paragraph{Fallback controls}
All nine injected primary failures produce independently audited V2
fallback. The three exception cases record \texttt{primary\_exception},
the three invalid-vector cases \texttt{primary\_independent\_audit\_failed},
and the three inconsistent-bound cases \texttt{native\_bound\_inconsistent}.
These outcomes are separate from the zero natural fallback count. They
establish handling of these post-preparation faults, not a guarantee of
availability or of V2 matching MIQP's objective when fallback is needed.
